FAQ

Asked and answered

The questions a careful donor should ask, answered the way we'd want them answered before giving our own money.

Is my donation tax-deductible?+

No. Not in the US, the UK, or anywhere else, and we'd rather tell you that in the first sentence than in the fine print.

US and UK charitable frameworks were not built for buying equipment for another country's military units. Some organizations thread that needle through carefully structured programs; the structures are slow and expensive, which is exactly what this fund exists not to be. Funds that blur the line get shut down, and their donors get burned.

If a structure ever becomes available that is both honest and workable, we will pursue it and announce it here first.

Is this an official IDF project?+

No. The fund is an independent initiative, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of the IDF or the State of Israel.

It is also not freelancing at the units' expense: needs are flagged by personnel inside the units, and every delivery is coordinated with and accepted by unit leadership. Nothing arrives unrequested and nothing arrives unannounced.

Why not just give to an established organization?+

You should, if what you want to fund is what they do: large organizations supporting Israel and its soldiers do essential work at a scale this fund will never touch.

This fund covers the opposite end of the spectrum: purchases too small, too specific, and too time-sensitive for any large organization's process. A ₪900 part is below everyone's radar except the unit that's stuck without it.

What will you buy, and what won't you?+

Will: 3D printers and filament, spare parts for equipment already in service, hand and power tools, batteries and power management, cables, connectors, mounts, and workshop consumables. Small, off-the-shelf, immediately useful.

Won't, ever: weapons, ammunition, weapon accessories, or anything that requires a defense or export license. This line is absolute. It keeps the fund legal in every jurisdiction it touches, and it keeps the mission what it says it is.

How do I know the money actually arrives?+

Every purchase is published after delivery: the item, the cost, and what it unblocked. Receipts are kept for all of it. The fund's books are small enough to read in one sitting, and they'll stay on this site.

We'll also say the uncomfortable part ourselves: the fund is new and has no track record yet. The first published purchases are how that changes. If you'd rather wait and watch the first reports land before giving, that is a completely reasonable position.

Who decides what gets funded?+

Needs come from personnel inside innovation-focused ground-forces units — the people actually blocked. Each one is verified with unit leadership before money moves. The fund then prioritizes by a simple test: which purchase unblocks the most work, soonest.

Can I earmark my donation for a specific item or unit?+

No. Pooled money is the mechanism: it's what lets a verified need be met in days. Earmarks would also require publishing the kind of specific-gap information we deliberately keep off the internet.

For a larger gift where you'd like a real conversation about where it goes, write to contact@defenseofinnovation.com.

Why is the site so vague about units and needs?+

Because a public list of what specific units lack is intelligence, and we refuse to produce it. We never publish unit designations, locations, personnel identities, faces, or open capability gaps. Purchases are described generically, after delivery. When in doubt, we leave it out.

How much goes to overhead?+

The fund has no staff, no office, and no paid fundraising. Payment-processor fees are the only cost taken out of donations; everything else that reaches the fund goes to purchases.

What happens to unspent money?+

It waits for the next verified need; that's the design. If the fund ever winds down, remaining money is spent down on the same categories of equipment through the same process, and the final ledger gets published like every other one.
