01 · The gap

Why a fund this small needs to exist

The IDF's procurement system does what military procurement systems do: it standardizes, it audits, it buys at scale. For vehicles, rations, and rifles, that rigor is the point. For a ₪4,000 printer that a unit's tinkerers want on a workbench this month, it is a wall. The request crawls from signature to signature until the moment has passed and the workaround has hardened into routine.

People inside innovation-focused units feel this constantly. They prototype, repair, and adapt equipment for the units around them, and what stops them is rarely knowledge or will. It is a missing item that any civilian could order before lunch.

02 · The model

Three steps, no machinery

01

You give to one pooled fund

No campaigns, no earmarks, no progress bars waiting to fill. Pooled money is what makes a six-day turnaround possible: when a need surfaces, the funds already exist. You give what a fix costs; we deploy it where it moves a unit forward fastest.

02

People inside the units flag what's blocking them

The fund grew out of direct working relationships with personnel in innovation-focused ground-forces units. Needs come from the people doing the work, not from a wishlist we invented. Every delivery is accepted knowingly by unit leadership.

03

We buy retail and hand it over

Off the shelf, receipt kept, delivered in person. Afterward the purchase is published here: what it was, what it cost, and what it unblocked, in terms that identify no unit, no location, and no soldier.

04 · Straight answers

A fund asking for money owes you blunt clarity

/Not tax-deductible

US and UK charitable frameworks were not built for buying equipment for another country's military units, and funds that pretend otherwise get shut down. This is a gift, not a deduction. If a formal structure ever becomes honest and workable, we will pursue it and announce it here.

/Not official

The fund is an independent initiative, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of the IDF. What keeps it grounded is simpler than a stamp: every delivery is coordinated with and accepted by unit leadership.

/No names, no faces

We never publish unit designations, locations, personnel identities, or specific capability gaps. Purchases are reported in generic terms, after delivery. When in doubt, we leave it out.

/No weapons

Nothing that fires, explodes, or requires a license. Printers, parts, tools, power. The boring equipment that lets capable people move.
