FAQ

Fair questions

The ones a careful donor should ask, answered without spin.

Is my donation tax-deductible?+

No. Not in the US, the UK, or anywhere else.

Charitable frameworks in those countries were not written for buying equipment for another country's military. A few large organizations manage it through carefully built programs; those structures take years and lawyers, which is the opposite of what this fund is for. We would rather stay small and fast and tell you the truth in the first sentence.

If a structure that is both honest and practical becomes available, it will be announced here first.

Is this an official IDF project?+

No. The fund is an independent initiative, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Israeli army or the State of Israel.

It also doesn't work around the units it serves: requests come from soldiers inside them, and every delivery is checked with and accepted by unit commanders.

Why not give to an established organization instead?+

If what you want to fund is what they do, you should. The large organizations supporting Israeli soldiers work at a scale this fund will never touch.

This fund covers what falls below their radar: a ₪900 part, a specific tool, a printer needed this month. Purchases too small and too time-sensitive for a large organization's process are exactly the ones that keep a unit's work moving.

What will you buy, and what won't you?+

Will: 3D printers and filament, spare parts for equipment in service, hand and power tools, batteries and chargers, connectors, cabling, mounts, and workshop consumables. Small, off the shelf, immediately useful.

Won't: weapons, ammunition, weapon accessories, or anything that requires a defense or export license. That rule keeps the fund legal everywhere it touches, and it keeps the mission what this site says it is.

How do I know the money actually arrives?+

The fund keeps receipts and records for every purchase, and the books are small enough to read in one sitting. What gets shared publicly is limited by security — the kinds of equipment bought and what they cost, rather than a full itemized display.

Behind that limit sits a named person, not a communications department. If you want more detail before giving, write and ask.

Who decides what gets funded?+

Needs are flagged by soldiers inside the units and verified with unit commanders before money moves. Between verified needs, priority goes to whichever purchase unblocks the most work soonest.

Can I earmark my donation?+

No. Pooled money is the mechanism that makes same-week purchases possible, and earmarking would require publishing exactly the kind of specific-gap information we keep off the internet.

For a larger gift where you want a real conversation about its use, write to contact@defenseofinnovation.com.

Why doesn't the site name the units?+

A public list of which units lack what would be useful to exactly the wrong readers. So unit names, base locations, soldiers' identities, and open equipment gaps never appear here, and what the fund shares about its purchases stays general.

How much goes to overhead?+

There is no staff, office, or paid fundraising to pay for. The card-processing fee is the only cost taken out of donations; the rest goes to purchases.

What happens to unspent money?+

It waits for the next verified need. If the fund ever winds down, whatever remains is spent on the same categories of equipment through the same process, and donors are told how it ended.
