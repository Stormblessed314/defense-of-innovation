About

An efficiency machine, kept small

The fund exists to do one narrow thing well: move small equipment to IDF ground-forces units at the speed a civilian takes for granted.

Where it comes from

Born on the workbench

Inside the IDF's ground forces there are units and people whose job is to solve problems with technology: prototype a fixture, repair what the field breaks, adapt off-the-shelf gear to a task nobody planned for. Watching them work, one pattern repeats. The blocker is almost never skill. It is a ₪900 part, a specific tool, a printer that the requisition system will deliver a quarter from now.

The Defense of Innovation Fund was set up to close exactly that gap. It is not trying to re-equip an army, and it is not a general war-effort charity. It is a short, fast line between a donor and a specific small purchase that lets capable people keep moving.

How it's built

Professional, without the apparatus

01

Run by a named person

The fund is run by Aryeh Lev Mason, working directly with personnel in innovation-focused IDF units. Nobody anonymous handles your money. As the fund grows, people from inside those units will take advisory roles, published here to the extent security allows.

02

Lean on purpose

No office, no staff, no fundraising apparatus. The fund's entire argument is speed and low friction; growing an administration would eat the thing it exists to deliver. Payment-processor fees are the only cost taken from donations.

03

Honest about structure

The fund currently operates as an independent initiative rather than a registered charity, and donations are not tax-deductible. Formal charitable frameworks were not designed for this kind of giving. If registration ever becomes both honest and workable, we'll pursue it and announce it here first.

04

Published, within limits

Every purchase is reported after delivery: item, cost, what it unblocked. The limits are security, not convenience. We never publish unit designations, locations, personnel identities, or specific capability gaps. When in doubt, we leave it out.
