The problem

Army purchasing was built for big orders

The Israeli army buys at national scale — vehicles, ammunition, food for hundreds of thousands of soldiers. That system is deliberate and slow, and for the big things the caution is right. The trouble is that a small purchase rides the same track: a ₪4,000 3D printer for a technical unit needs the same signatures and budget lines as a fleet order, and it can arrive months after the moment it was needed.

The soldiers this fund serves repair equipment the field breaks, print replacement parts, and adapt off-the-shelf gear for military work. What holds them up is usually not the hard part of the job. It is a small item, in stock at a store down the road, waiting in a queue.

That queue is what this fund exists to skip.

How it works

From your card to a unit, in four steps

Donations go into one pooled fund

There are no campaigns and no earmarks. Pooling is what makes the speed possible — when a request is verified, the money is already there.

Soldiers flag what's missing

Requests come from soldiers serving in the units the fund works with. Each one is checked with the unit's commanders before anything is bought.

We buy it at retail

Off the shelf, usually inside a week, and handed over through the unit's own chain of command.

We report back

Every shekel is accounted for and receipts are kept. We share what security allows: the kinds of equipment bought, what they cost, and what they got moving.

Before you give

Four things you should know

Donations are not tax-deductible

There is no charity registration behind this fund. US and UK charitable law was not written for buying equipment for a foreign military, and we would rather stay fast and say so than stretch the truth. Treat a donation as a personal gift.

The fund is independent of the IDF

It is not run, endorsed, or funded by the army. Deliveries go through unit commanders and nothing is bought without their sign-off, but this is a private initiative and it says so plainly.

Some details stay off the site

Unit names, base locations, soldiers' identities, and what a specific unit is currently missing are never published — a list like that would be useful to the wrong people. Purchases are described in general terms, after delivery.

No weapons, ever

The fund buys printers, parts, tools, and batteries. It does not buy weapons, ammunition, weapon accessories, or anything that requires a defense or export license.

Who runs it

A named person, not an organization

The fund is run by Aryeh Lev Mason, working directly with soldiers in the Israeli units it serves. There is no staff and no office, and the card-processing fee is the only cost taken out of a donation.

Questions before giving are welcome, and the person who answers is the person who runs the fund: contact@defenseofinnovation.com
