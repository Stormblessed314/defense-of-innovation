About

One narrow job, done fast

Get small equipment to Israeli units at the speed a civilian takes for granted.

Origin

Where it started

Inside the Israeli ground forces there are units whose work is technical: repairing what the field breaks, printing replacement parts, adapting commercial gear to jobs nobody planned for. Spend time around them and one pattern repeats. The soldiers are rarely stuck on the hard part of the job. They are stuck on a ₪900 part, a specific tool, a printer the requisition system will deliver a quarter from now.

This fund was set up to close that specific gap. It is not trying to re-equip an army and it is not a general war-effort charity. It buys the small thing, this week, so the work keeps moving.

Structure

How it's built

A named person, not an organization

The fund is run by Aryeh Lev Mason, working directly with soldiers in the Israeli units it serves. As it grows, advisors from inside those units will be named here, as far as security allows.

Almost nothing spent on itself

There is no staff and no office, and nobody is paid to fundraise. The card-processing fee is the only cost taken out of a donation; the rest goes to purchases.

No charity registration, said plainly

The fund operates as an independent initiative, and donations are not tax-deductible. Charitable frameworks in the US and UK were not written for foreign military equipment, and we would rather be clear about that than creative. If a workable structure appears, it will be announced here first.

Accountable, within security limits

The fund keeps receipts and records for everything it buys, and shares what security allows — the kinds of equipment and what they cost. Unit names, base locations, soldiers' identities, and open equipment gaps never appear on this site.
